What it takes off your desk

Six kinds of work. Answering the phone is one of them.

Most of what fills a small business's day is the same few jobs, done again. Each of these runs today.

01

Answer

The phone out of hours and on a busy line, WhatsApp messages, voice notes. In your business's name, from your own facts and rules.

02

Write up

A note after every contact: who it was, what they wanted, what was promised. The text to your customer. The call back you now owe.

03

Read

Photos, PDFs, letters, timesheets, recordings and your inbox. Sorted, the figures pulled out, and what it was unsure of said plainly.

04

Look up

Your own listings and records, parts and live prices, every diary you keep, journeys, the web.

05

Draft

Estimates, invoices, replies and decks, with the arithmetic done in code. Yours to check, change and send.

06

Chase and watch

Reminders, mail that can't wait, diary clashes, meeting follow-ups, a call made for you from a brief you approved.

Your own workflows

Write the simple ones yourself. We build the rest.

Describe what should happen and when, as you would to a member of staff. Your deputy names it, draws how it would run, and builds it from the parts it already has. You turn it on.

When a workflow needs a part it lacks, such as a way into a supplier's site, the drawing says so. What it has can go on today; the rest comes to us as one request, with your words attached.

Your deputy never writes its own code. What you build is made only of tested parts, and anything new is built, tested and signed by a person before it runs.

You stay in charge

One word from your phone.

Every workflow stops where a person should decide. Reply ok to accept a draft, drop to bin it, or just say what to change and it comes back as a new version. From WhatsApp, a text, or your own pages.

Some things are not deputable: pricing, hiring, contractual commitments, and any complaint with real consequences. Those stay with your people, always.

01

A named person approves it.

Anything a customer will see waits at review until one of your people says ok.

02

Your ok covers what you read.

Change anything and it comes back as a new version, to be approved again.

03

Every message goes once.

If we can't tell whether one was delivered, a person decides before it is tried again.

04

Outside text can't give orders.

A caller's words or an email can inform a draft. They can never make your deputy send or change anything.

These four are rules in the code, each held by its own automated test. They are not instructions we hope a model follows.

It learns how you work

The more you use it, the more it works your way.

Each night your deputy looks back over what you changed. Make the same change to its drafts three times in five and it proposes a lesson. Ask for the same work the same way twice and it proposes a skill.

It always proposes the smallest change that does the job, and nothing about how it works changes without your say.
