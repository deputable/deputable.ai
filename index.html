Smart Call Handling

What happens when the phone rings.

Your deputy covers for your team and backs it up. It takes no one's place. Where a step belongs to a person, it stops and waits.

Before the first word

It reads up.

Who is calling. Your staff are greeted by name. A customer is answered as the business.
What happened last time. The last call, the notes, what was promised.
Your rules. Hours, services and standing rules in your own words: “never give a price”.
On the call

It answers as you.

Says what it is. The greeting names your business, says it is an AI assistant and that the call is recorded.
Answers what it safely can from your own facts, and says so when it can't.
Takes it down properly. Who, the number, what is wrong, what they want.
They ask for a person. It holds the caller and rings your mobile. If you can't take it, a call back is promised.
After the call

It writes it up.

A note on your WhatsApp as the call ends: who rang, why, what was promised.
Your customer gets it in writing, by text, only if they said yes.
A promised call back becomes an item that waits until it is done.
Any draft waits for your ok. An estimate, a reply, an invoice. Nothing goes on its own.

You stay in charge

One word from your phone.

Drafts wait for you. Reply ok to accept one, drop to bin it, or just say what to change and it comes back as a new version. From WhatsApp, a text, or your own pages.

Some things are not deputable: pricing, hiring, contractual commitments, and any complaint with real consequences. Those stay with your people, always.

01

A named person approves it.

Anything a customer will see waits at review until one of your people says ok.

02

Your ok covers what you read.

Change anything and it comes back as a new version, to be approved again.

03

Every message goes once.

If we can't tell whether one was delivered, a person decides before it is tried again.

04

Outside text can't give orders.

A caller's words or an email can inform a draft. They can never make your deputy send or change anything.

These four are rules in the code, each held by its own automated test. They are not instructions we hope a model follows.

It learns how you work

The more you use it, the more it works your way.

Each night your deputy looks back over what you changed. When you make the same change to its drafts three times in five, it proposes a lesson. You keep it, make it a rule, or dismiss it for good.

Nothing about how it works changes without your say.
